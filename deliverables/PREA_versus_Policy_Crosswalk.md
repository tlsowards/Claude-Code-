# PREA versus Policy: a standard-by-standard crosswalk

Sacramento County Probation Department, Youth Detention Facility.
Gap register Revision 4. 83 requirements assessed against 17 departmental policies.

**This is not legal advice.** It is a policy-to-standard comparison prepared for internal remediation planning. Statutory questions route to County Counsel.

## 1. What this document does

The gap register records, for each requirement, what the department has. This document sets the requirement and the departmental provision next to each other so the divergence is visible on its face, and then inverts the view so each of the 17 reviewed policies can be worked one at a time.

Three divergence classes are used throughout.

- **Conflict.** The policy states a rule, and the rule is wrong. This is the worst class, because staff following policy are out of compliance by doing what they were told. Twelve requirements sit here.
- **Not Addressed.** The documents reviewed contain no provision on the point. Sixteen requirements sit here, ten of which cite no departmental document at all.
- **Partial.** A provision exists and does part of the work. Forty requirements sit here, and this is where most of the remediation labor is.

Findings state what the documents reviewed contain. Where a requirement is recorded as not addressed, that means no provision appeared in the 17 documents, not that the practice does not occur. Several findings will close on production of the documents listed as outstanding.

## 3. Class A divergence: policy states the wrong rule

Twelve requirements where a departmental document affirmatively states a rule that contradicts federal or California law, or contradicts another departmental document. These are listed first because they are the only class where compliant staff behavior produces non-compliance.

### Conflict 1. Categorical LGBTQI housing (classification S-8)

*Register row 36.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.342(c); 15 CCR 1352(e). LGBTI residents shall not be placed in particular housing, bed, or other assignments solely on the basis of that identification or status.

**The policy says.** OO 1352 II.M creates classification S-8 for any LGBTQI youth, and III.I requires single-room housing at all times for S-8.

**Why it matters.** Also contradicts OO 1352's own Purpose and Scope paragraph, PREA Policy III.E, and OO 1352.5 III.H. The chronology is damaging: OO 1352.5 stated the correct rule effective 03/2019, and S-8 was created 12/2019 and revised 02/2020.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 1: rescind the S-8 single-room mandate.

### Conflict 2. Categorical single-room housing for transgender and intersex youth

*Register row 37.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.342(d) to (g); 15 CCR 1352.5(a) to (e). Housing and program assignments are decided case by case.

**The policy says.** OO 1352.5 III.I gives all transgender and intersex youth a single room.

**Why it matters.** The same categorical defect as conflict 1 but more defensible: there is a privacy rationale and program access is preserved at III.K. It still contradicts III.B, III.F, and III.H of the same order.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 2: convert to a presumptive, documented, individualized outcome.

### Conflict 3. Child abuse reporting timeline

*Register row 48.*

**The law requires.** PC 11166(a). Telephone report immediately or as soon as practicably possible, written report within 36 hours on DOJ form SS 8572.

**The policy says.** PREA Policy V.A.4 and OO 1453 I.A.9 both state that sexual assault is reported to Child Protective Services verbally within 24 hours.

**Why it matters.** The error appears in two documents, and OO 1453 restated it on a 12/2019 revision. Code of Conduct VII.A.1 states the rule correctly, so the department is not uniform against itself.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 3: correct the standard and relocate it to a new department-wide General Order.

### Conflict 4. Cross-facility notification window

*Register row 52.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.363(b). Notify the head of the facility where the abuse allegedly occurred within 72 hours.

**The policy says.** PREA Policy V.A.7 allows 14 days.

**Why it matters.** A straight timeline error. The 14-day figure appears to have been carried over from the attorney notification timeline in 115.361(e)(3).

**Remediation.** Recommended change 4: correct 14 days to 72 hours.

### Conflict 5. Discipline for allegations found false

*Register row 64.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.378(f) protects a good-faith report based on a reasonable belief even where the investigation does not substantiate it. 115.352(g) permits discipline only on a showing of bad faith.

**The policy says.** PREA Policy XIV.A permits discipline where an investigation determines the allegations were false.

**Why it matters.** A false-allegations rule stated at this breadth suppresses reporting, which is the behavior the standard is written to protect.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 5: rewrite to a bad-faith standard.

### Conflict 6. Victimization treated as an indicator of abusiveness

*Register row 80.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.341(c). Prior victimization and prior abusiveness are screened as distinct risk factors.

**The policy says.** The note at OO 1352 II.F.2 states that a history of being a victim of molest, arson, or cruelty to animals should alert the Classification Officer to possible sexually inappropriate tendencies.

**Why it matters.** This instructs staff to treat a youth's own sexual victimization as a predictor of sexual abusiveness, which is the inference 115.341(c) separates.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 6: delete the inference, split the code, and add PC 287 to the S-4 High criteria.

### Conflict 7. Records retention

*Register row 59.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.371(j). Retain investigation reports for as long as the alleged abuser is employed or incarcerated, plus five years.

**The policy says.** Internal Affairs XI.C.1 sets five years. PREA Policy XVIII.B sets ten years.

**Why it matters.** Three different rules govern the same records. AB 452 eliminated the limitations period for childhood sexual assault occurring on or after 01/01/2024, so the shortest of the three is the one that will be tested.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 7: adopt a single reconciled retention schedule.

### Conflict 8. Staffing ratios

*Register row 6.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.313(c). At least 1:8 during waking hours and 1:16 during sleeping hours, security staff only, required since 10/01/2017.

**The policy says.** OO 1321 I.B.1 to 2 sets 1:10 waking and 1:30 sleeping.

**Why it matters.** The county standard mirrors the Title 15 floor. 15 CCR 1301 expressly permits exceeding it. Verify rosters and post assignments before assuming the practice matches the written ratio in either direction.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 9: verify against rosters and build the 115.313(a) staffing plan.

### Conflict 9. Duty assigned to a position that does not exist

*Register row 2.*

**The law requires.** 28 CFR 115.311(b). Designate an upper-level, agency-wide PREA Coordinator.

**The policy says.** OO 1453 I.A.15 directs the sexual abuse incident review report to the PREA Coordinator, a position the department does not have.

**Why it matters.** Two consequences. The required report has nowhere to go, so the review terminates in a dead letter. And this is written departmental acknowledgment, dated 2019, that the role is needed.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 8: designate a PREA Coordinator and a facility Compliance Manager.

### Conflict 10. Policy age and biennial review

*Register row 74.*

**The law requires.** 15 CCR 1324. The policy and procedure manual is administratively reviewed at least every two years and updated as necessary.

**The policy says.** The PREA Policy was last revised 04/25/2013. The Internal Affairs policy is effective 01/11/2011 with no revision shown.

**Why it matters.** The PREA Policy predates the 01/01/2019 Title 15 rewrite that added 1350.5, 1352.5, 1324(n), and 1361(h), and predates the renumbering of PC 288a to PC 287.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 3 in part, and the full rewrite at likely next task 3.

### Conflict 11. Obsolete statutory citations

*Register row 78.*

**The law requires.** PC 287. SB 1494 renumbered PC 288a to PC 287 effective 01/01/2019.

**The policy says.** PREA Policy I.I.1 still cites PC 288a. OO 1352 II.G.1 omits 287 from the S-4 High criteria entirely, so a 287 adjudication does not meet the enumerated criteria. OO 1352 II.C.3 references DJJ, which closed 06/30/2023.

**Why it matters.** The S-4 omission is the operative one: it is not a citation cosmetic, it changes who gets classified.

**Remediation.** Recommended change 6.

### Conflict 12. Intake policy substantively obsolete

*Register row 79.*

**The law requires.** 15 CCR 1350 and 1350.5. Intake and admittance policy must reflect current law and carry the admittance elements added in 2019.

**The policy says.** Detention and Intake Responsibility (J-3.4) references CYA and CYA parolees throughout, contains no PREA or sexual abuse screening content, and does not reference OO 1350.5.

**Why it matters.** CYA became the Division of Juvenile Justice in 2005 and closed entirely on 06/30/2023 under SB 823.

**Remediation.** Rewrite rather than amend.

### Responsive Planning

#### 16. 28 CFR 115.321(a)-(b); 15 CCR 1452, 1453

| | |
|---|---|
| Status | Partial |
| Priority | High |
| Departmental provision | OO 1453 I.A.3, I.A.5, I.A.11; PREA Policy VIII.A |

**The standard requires.** Follow a uniform evidence protocol that maximizes usable physical evidence, developmentally appropriate for youth and adapted from the DOJ Office on Violence Against Women national protocol for sexual assault medical forensic examinations or a comparable authoritative protocol.

**Divergence.** OO 1453 supplies real evidence procedure: crime scene sealing, prevention of showering, collection of toilet tissue, and at I.A.11 a fallback protocol where the Sheriff's Department or CSI cannot respond immediately, under which the Duty Supervisor photographs the scene, gloves up, collects and marks evidence with date, time, location, ownership and description, seals and logs it, and places it in the evidence locker at Intake and Booking. That is a workable chain of custody procedure. Two concerns. First, the standard requires a uniform evidence protocol that is developmentally appropriate for youth and adapted from the DOJ Office on Violence Against Women national protocol or a comparable authoritative source; OO 1453 is a locally drafted procedure with no such derivation and no youth-specific adaptation. Second, I.A.11 authorizes a Duty Supervisor to collect sexual assault evidence with no stated training prerequisite. 15 CCR 1452 requires forensic medical evidence be collected by appropriately trained medical personnel not responsible for the youth's ongoing care. Scene evidence is not forensic medical evidence and the two are governed differently, but an untrained supervisor collecting evidence in a sexual assault case is a cross-examination target and a chain of custody risk. Add a training requirement and derive the protocol from an authoritative source.

*Change log: REVISED in 3: OO 1453 evidence procedure credited; training prerequisite and protocol derivation identified as the residual gaps.*

Sexual misconduct is a major rule violation at II.B.4, but where the recommended consequence is point loss, loss of commissary privilege, early bed, loss of bank points, or demotion to a lower level, no hearing is required by the order's own terms, whereas the standard requires the formal process for any discipline imposed for resident-on-resident sexual abuse. Second, sexual misconduct is listed as a major rule violation and is nowhere defined, so nothing distinguishes coerced from non-coerced sexual activity between residents, and nothing carves out sexual contact with a staff member, which the standard permits to be disciplined only on a finding that the staff member did not consent. That omission is serious in California, where PC 289.6 makes sexual activity between staff and a confined person criminal irrespective of purported consent, so a youth disciplined for sexual misconduct arising from staff contact would be punished for being the victim of a crime. Third, no provision requires consideration of whether a mental disability or mental illness contributed to the behavior when determining sanctions. IV.B.2 provides procedural accommodations at the hearing, which is a different thing, and the Purpose paragraph's trauma-informed language is permissive, using should rather than shall. Fourth, the standard requires sanctions commensurate with the resident's disciplinary history and consistent with those imposed for comparable offenses by residents with similar histories. The Purpose paragraph's least restrictive level language and the appeal ground at V.A.3 for discipline different from that received by others for the same offense reach part of this, but neither states the history-informed comparison. Confirm subsection lettering within 115.378 against the CFR before any of this is quoted in a deliverable; only (f) is verified here.

*Change log: MATERIALLY REVISED in 4: OO 1390/1391 reviewed. The formal disciplinary process the standard requires now largely exists, so remediation shifts from drafting a process to extending one. The XIV.A conflict is unchanged. Two new defects: the due process trigger is limited to Program Separation, and sexual misconduct is undefined with no staff-consent carve-out.*

## 6. How to use this against the register

`prea-register.csv` remains the working record. This document is generated from it by `tools/build_crosswalk.py`, so it is a view, not a second source of truth. When a finding changes, edit the CSV, record the change in the `change_log` column, and regenerate. The `owner`, `target_date`, and `disposition` columns are intentionally empty and are what turn the register into the corrective action plan.
