## Step 1. Ages, before anything else

Age decides the CPS column. Settle it first, because it changes the answer before you even look at the conduct. Residents 18 and over are not children for CANRA, whatever the court jurisdiction, and the department does not treat them as dependent adults.

| Who is involved | What happens to the CPS column |
|---|---|
| Both under 18 | Use the chart as written. |
| One under 18, one over | **Still reports**, on every tier the chart says YES. **Never run consent analysis.** A minor cannot lawfully consent with an adult, so apparent willingness changes nothing. |
| Both 18 or over | **No CPS report on any tier.** No child, so no CANRA. PREA is unchanged. The external report goes to the **Sheriff**, not CPS, and only for sexual assault. |

## Step 2. Find the conduct

Two separate tests. They line up on touching and come apart everywhere else. Answering one never answers the other.

| Tier | What happened | Is it PREA? | CPS report? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | One sexualized comment. No contact, no exposure, no threat. | Not PREA | **No** |
| **2** | Sexualized comments or gestures, repeated and unwelcome, at the same youth. | PREA sexual harassment | **No** |
| **3** | Exposes genitals. No masturbation, no contact. | Harassment only if repeated | **No. Read tier 4 first** |
| **4** | Masturbates in view of another youth. | Harassment only if repeated | **YES if the youth who saw it is under 18** |
| **5** | Touches genitals, anus, groin, breast, inner thigh or buttocks. Through clothing counts. | PREA SEXUAL ABUSE | **YES if either resident is under 18** |
| **6** | Oral, anal or penetrative contact, attempted or completed. Or anything above by force, threat or coercion. | PREA SEXUAL ABUSE | **YES if either resident is under 18** |

## Step 3. Check for a flip

Any one of these moves the incident up and forces a deliberate CPS call, whatever tier the conduct looked like.

- Force, threat, coercion or leverage of any kind, including debt, protection or commissary.
- Any sign it happened before, reported or not.
- The target is under 14.
- A significant age, size, developmental or cognitive gap between the two.
- The target discloses prior sexual abuse, inside or outside the facility.
- Reason to suspect the youth who acted has himself been abused. That is a separate report about a different child.
- A pattern by the same youth toward more than one target.
- **Any staff involvement at all.** Off this chart entirely, and it reports regardless of tier.

## Step 4. Act

Cumulative. Each block adds to the one above it.

**Every tier, including tier 1**

- Separate the youth. Check the target is safe.
- Staff tells the Duty Supervisor immediately.
- Incident report naming both youth, **the words used verbatim** and the target's response verbatim.
- Record your classification and the reason, including a decision not to make a CPS report.
- Treat it as classification information on both youth, and check the target's history for a repeat.

**Add at tier 2 and above**

- Log it as a PREA incident.
- Decide whether the target is at substantial risk of imminent sexual abuse. If yes, act to protect now, separately from any investigation.
- Reassess classification and housing for both youth.
- If anyone reported it, start retaliation monitoring: at least 90 days, with checks.

**Add at tier 4, where a CPS report is due**

- **Telephone the mandated report immediately**, or as soon as practicably possible. Written report on form SS 8572 within 36 hours.
- Do not wait for the investigation, the end of shift, or anyone's approval.
- The duty belongs to the employee who formed the suspicion. Telling you does not discharge it, and no supervisor may impede it.

**Add at tiers 5 and 6**

- Treat it as a sexual abuse allegation from the first moment.
- **Preserve evidence.** No showering, changing or cleaning up. Secure the area.
- Refer for investigation. Every allegation gets one. There is no discretion to decide one is unnecessary.
- Tier 6 adds: law enforcement notification, forensic exam off site, and notice to the parent or guardian and counsel.

## Step 5. Write the classification down

Put this block in the incident report every time, including when you decide it is nothing. PREA sexual harassment requires conduct that is repeated, and repetition only exists if the first one was written down.

```
Tier: ____    Conduct in one line: ______________________________
Ages: both under 18 / one under one over / both 18 or over
PREA call: Not PREA  /  Sexual harassment  /  SEXUAL ABUSE
Flip factors present: none / ____________________________________
CPS report: made at ______ by ______  /  not made, because ______
Protection and housing action taken: ____________________________
Retaliation monitoring started: yes / no / not applicable
Supervisor: ______________________  Date and time: ______________
```

## Step 6. What happens after you call it PREA

Classifying it is the start, not the end. This is the sequence OO 1453 and the PREA Policy set out, in order. Provenance: OO 1453 has never been produced to this review, so these section numbers and their content trace to the gap register, which traces to the earlier review that did read the order. Confirm against the order before relying on a number.

**1. First response, immediately**  
*OO 1453 I.A.1, I.A.3 to I.A.5*

- Separate victim and suspect. **No contact between them.**
- Protect the crime scene: the youth's room, the shower area, the laundry room. Seal it off and deny access to other staff and youth.
- **Neither youth showers, changes or cleans up.**
- Keep both out of the bathroom where you can. If one is used, collect the toilet tissue as evidence.

**2. Initial statement, and the trap in it**  
*OO 1453 I.A.6; WIC 625.6*

- **I.A.6: "The duty supervisor shall take an initial statement from the victim and suspect."** This is directed, not optional, and it is yours.
- **I.A.6 also says all suspects "shall be read a Miranda Warning, prior to any questioning."**
- **STOP THERE IF THE SUSPECT IS 17 OR UNDER.** WIC 625.6 requires that youth to consult legal counsel, in person, by telephone or by video, BEFORE any custodial interrogation and BEFORE waiving Miranda. **The consultation cannot be waived.** The order predates that statute by more than a year and does not mention it. Get the consultation, then question, and write down that you did.
- Note what this does not cover: canvassing other residents to test whether the allegation is true. The order does not direct that, and it is what belongs to a trained investigator.

**3. Notify, immediately**  
*OO 1453 I.A.8 to I.A.10; PC 11166(a)*

- **Duty Supervisor notifies the Sheriff's Department immediately** once it is believed a sexual assault occurred.
- **CPS by telephone immediately**, where the age chart says a report is due. Written report on SS 8572 within 36 hours. Note: PREA Policy V.A.4 and OO 1453 I.A.9 both say 24 hours. **That is wrong and the law controls.**
- The reporting or investigating officer briefs the responding Sheriff's officers and provides an interview room.

**4. Evidence, if the Sheriff cannot respond at once**  
*OO 1453 I.A.11*

- Duty Supervisor photographs the scene, gloves up, collects and marks each item with date, time, location, ownership and description.
- Seal it, log it, place it in the evidence locker at Intake and Booking.

**5. Medical and advocate**  
*OO 1453 I.A.13; PREA Policy VIII.A and XV.A; PC 264.2, PC 679.04*

- **Evidentiary examination happens off site**, at a health facility separate from the custodial facility.
- Victim advocate, in most cases from a local Rape Crisis Center. The youth has a right to an advocate or support person during the medical examination and during investigatory interviews.
- Crisis counseling.

**6. Investigation**  
*28 CFR 115.371(a); OO 1453 I.A.14*

- **Every allegation gets an administrative or criminal investigation.** There is no threshold of plausibility and no discretion to decide one is unnecessary.
- Investigative reports route to the Supervising District Attorney and the Sheriff's Sexual Assaults Unit.

**7. Incident review, at the conclusion**  
*OO 1453 I.A.15; PREA Policy V.A.8; 28 CFR 115.386*

- Upper management conducts a review, with input from supervisors, investigators and medical or mental health staff.
- A written report is generated and forwarded to the Department Chief.
- The review is required for **unsubstantiated** allegations too. Only an **unfounded** finding switches it off.

**8. Ongoing, and the part most often missed**  
*28 CFR 115.362, 115.367; PREA Policy XVIII.B*

- Reassess classification and housing for both youth.
- **Retaliation monitoring for at least 90 days** for anyone who reported or was the target, with periodic checks.
- Records retained, and the incident enters the department's data.

### Four things current policy will not tell you

- **I.A.6 is a year and a half out of date and it can cost you the case.** It directs a Miranda warning before questioning a suspect and says nothing about the WIC 625.6 counsel consultation, which has been mandatory and unwaivable for youth 17 and under since 01/01/2021. The order was last revised 12/09/2019. Courts weigh noncompliance on admissibility, and weigh a willful violation against the officer's credibility.
- **The CPS timeline in policy is wrong.** PREA Policy V.A.4 and OO 1453 I.A.9 say 24 hours. PC 11166(a) says immediately. Follow the law. The exposure for a late report falls on the employee.
- **The incident review report has nowhere to go.** OO 1453 I.A.15 directs it to "the PREA Coordinator," a position the department does not have. Send it to the Department Chief and say in the report that the Coordinator role is vacant.
- **There is no retaliation monitoring program.** 115.367 requires a designated monitor and at least 90 days. Nothing in current policy sets one up, so if you do not run it yourself it does not happen.
- **Cross-facility notification is wrong.** If the youth says the abuse happened at another facility, PREA Policy V.A.7 gives 14 days. The standard is **72 hours**.

## Who does what, and where you stop

Classification and protection are yours. Fact finding is not. The distinction matters most in the middle of the chart, tiers 2 through 5, where there is no criminal callout and it feels like nobody else is coming. Somebody else is supposed to be coming. ### The stop-point, corrected against OO 1453

- **Corrected on 10/29.** An earlier version of this matrix told supervisors they do not conduct fact finding and do not interview. **OO 1453 I.A.6 says otherwise:** "The duty supervisor shall take an initial statement from the victim and suspect." The order had not been produced when that was written. The stop-point moves; it does not disappear.
- You classify. You protect. You preserve. **You take the initial statement from the victim and the suspect, because I.A.6 directs it.** You notify. You document. **Then you stop and hand off.**
- **What is still on the far side of the line:** canvassing further witnesses to find out whether the allegation is true. Nothing in the order directs that, and it is what belongs to a trained investigator under 115.334. Taking a statement from the two people involved is first response. Working the unit to test the account is investigation.
- **Anonymity is the practical test.** If residents asked not to be identified, do not go and find them. Identifying them burns the witnesses and creates the retaliation exposure 115.367 exists to prevent, and it is not something I.A.6 asks of you.

### Two things the department has to fix, one urgent

**URGENT, AND IT IS A LEGAL EXPOSURE ON THE SUPERVISOR PERSONALLY.** I.A.6 directs a Miranda warning before questioning a suspect. It says nothing about **WIC 625.6**, which since 01/01/2021 requires a youth **17 or younger** to consult legal counsel, in person, by telephone or by video, **before** a custodial interrogation and **before** waiving Miranda, and which provides that the consultation **may not be waived**. OO 1453 was last revised 12/09/2019, more than a year before that took effect. WIC 625.6(b) directs courts to weigh noncompliance when deciding whether the youth's statements come in, and to weigh a willful violation against the officer's credibility under Evidence Code 780. Until I.A.6 is amended, get the 625.6 consultation before any questioning of a suspect aged 17 or under, and record that you did. **Second, and structural:** 115.371(a) requires an administrative or criminal investigation for every allegation. The Sheriff covers the criminal branch. Nothing names who runs the administrative branch for youth-on-youth allegations, and Internal Affairs is written for employee misconduct. Register rows 60 and 91.

**If you are genuinely unsure, report.** The CANRA threshold is reasonable suspicion, which is deliberately low. Reporters have immunity. Failing to report is a crime. The risk is asymmetric on purpose, so classify fast where the chart is clear and report where it is not.
