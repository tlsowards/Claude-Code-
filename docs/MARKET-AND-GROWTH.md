# Market landscape and growth plan

Written November 5, 2026 from web research. Prices and claims come from the vendors' own pages unless noted, and should be re-checked before being quoted to anyone.

## Who is competing for the same sign-ups

Three groups compete for the attention, email addresses, and training budgets this app is after.

### 1. Free legal update publishers (compete for attention and inbox space)

| Who | What they publish | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lexipol, Xiphos (Ken Wallentine) | Monthly national criminal procedure and use of force newsletter, plus frequent Police1 and Lexipol blog pieces on individual cases | Free for public safety officers, educators, and public attorneys. Policing-focused. Lexipol also owns Police1 and Corrections1. |
| LLRMI (Jack Ryan) | Frequent legal update posts on search, seizure, use of force, and jail liability; Supreme Court roundups | Posts dated into November 2026. One item dated October 20, 2026 is titled "Accidental Shooting of a Hostage: 9th Circuit Ruling". The October 25 brief in this app reported only Astorga from the Ninth Circuit that week, so that item is worth checking. |
| Daigle Law Group | Legal update blog, on-demand courses, annual Use of Force Summit | Attorney-led, East Coast based, national audience. |
| CPOA | Annual Legislative and Legal Digest, quarterly journal, recorded legislative update webinar ($135 members, $185 non-members) | California-specific, annual cadence, policing audience. |
| CopWare | California Peace Officers Legal Sourcebook, updated about every 60 days, $240 with a year of revisions | Reference book, not a digest. |
| Consumer apps: StreetSense, Justified, Case Law Pro Cop | Paid phone apps with statutes and case law; Justified uses AI-generated summaries | No named authority behind the summaries, no training application, no corrections or juvenile angle. |

### 2. Podcasts

No weekly use of force case law podcast hosted by a California corrections or probation expert was found. The closest are The Briefing Room (episodic, general case law), LRIS First Thursday (public sector labor law), Law of Self Defense (civilian self-defense), and older FLETC use of force podcasts. The Use of Force Case Law Update has an open lane.

### 3. Training platforms (compete for agency budgets and STC or POST hours)

| Who | Offer | Price signal |
|---|---|---|
| CorrectionsOne Academy (Lexipol) | Up to 69 BSCC STC-approved online courses; markets that it covers all 24 required hours | Annual agency subscription priced by agency size. The most direct STC competitor. |
| Police1 Academy (Lexipol) | 1,000+ training videos, 175 accredited courses, approved for CE in several states | Annual agency subscription by size. |
| Vector Solutions (TargetSolutions, PRISM) | 250+ hours of online law enforcement training, POST integration through Acadis | Enterprise pricing. Once offered a free 1-hour POST use of force and de-escalation course under a POST grant. |
| Calibre Press | Street Survival seminars, Constitutional Use of Force course, online and in person | About $219 per officer for the online seminar; 30-day agency rentals. |
| Force Science Institute | Certification course, Force Encounters course, research newsletter | $1,695 in person, $1,399 online. Human factors authority, not case law. |
| Savage Training Group (Scott Savage, California) | POST-certified Use of Force Decision-Making and Legal Update (8 hours), supervisor courses, online PC 835a course | $299 for the legal update workshop, $389 to $459 for two-day courses. The closest California analog, aimed at police rather than probation and corrections. |
| CSU Long Beach | 24-hour POST Use of Force Train the Trainer | University pricing. |
| CPOC | Short STC online courses for probation | About $45 for 2 STC hours. |
| Blue to Gold (Anthony Bandiero) | Search and seizure courses, subscription video library, Legal Training Hotline, instructor certification, an app in development | $179 advanced course; subscription library. A good model of a single-expert brand that scaled. |

BSCC reports 151 agencies in the STC program and 262 private STC providers. The training provider manual references a tuition limit of $28.75 per hour; the April 2026 BSCC report proposes replacing the tuition cap with a maximum training cost. Sowards pricing at $65 to $80 per officer for a 4-hour course is $16 to $20 per hour, well under either figure.

## Where Sowards Consulting is different

1. **Audience nobody else serves weekly.** Every national publisher writes for patrol. The weekly brief reads each case for the floor, the housing unit, the juvenile hall, and the probation field contact, and says plainly when a convicted-prisoner case does not carry over to juveniles or pretrial detainees.
2. **Every case ends with "how to train it."** Competitors end with the holding. Trainers have to do the translation themselves.
3. **Federal first, then California, then application.** The 835a layer is built in, so California officers are never left training to the most forgiving federal circuit.
4. **Verification flags.** Saying "I could not read the district court opinion" in print is rare and builds trust with the people who testify.
5. **Price.** Free weekly content next to STC courses at a fraction of platform subscriptions, with free previews.
6. **One expert, on camera, in the courtroom.** An STC-certified trainer who is also a retained expert witness in custody and PREA cases is a credential none of the platforms can put a face on.

## Growth plan

The pattern that works for single-expert brands in this space (Blue to Gold, Wallentine, Branca) is the same: a free, reliable, weekly thing that practitioners forward, a face attached to it, and a paid product one click away. The plan below is ordered by effort.

### Built into the app now

- **Roll call brief.** Every weekly brief has a "Print roll call brief" button that produces a one-page table: case, what it means, train it, plus the week's briefing room points and the app link. Sergeants read it at briefing, training coordinators post it, and every copy carries the address.
- **Share button.** Each brief has "Share with your training sergeant," which uses the phone's share sheet.
- **Resource center as a reason to return.** Police1 videos, Corrections1, the legal update publishers, and the California instructors are linked. Trainers bookmark the page that collects everything.

### Weeks 1 to 4: distribution that costs nothing but time

1. **Sunday night email.** Send the brief Sunday evening so it lands before Monday roll call. Subject line is the week's first application point, not the case names. MailerLite is already in use for the de-escalation card.
2. **Podcast directories.** Submit the feed to Apple Podcasts and Spotify. The app's episode pages already link to the written brief, so listeners become readers.
3. **Probation training coordinators.** The Dropbox contact list of California probation departments is the first mailing: one email offering the roll call brief and free agency-wide subscription, with a reply-to for course previews. Fifty-eight counties, one coordinator each.
4. **Associations Dr. Sowards already belongs to or teaches for.** CAFOP, SCPA, SRPSTC, CSU Sacramento and Chico classes. Students and members install the app in the room; a QR code on the last slide of every class.
5. **LinkedIn.** Post one case per weekday from the brief, 150 words, with the "train it" paragraph and a link. Case names are what attorneys and trainers search.

### Months 2 to 3: content that compounds

6. **Train-it video shorts.** One 60-second vertical video per case, read by the existing HeyGen avatar, for YouTube Shorts, Instagram, and LinkedIn. The de-escalation YouTube launch plan in Dropbox already has the thumbnail and outro templates.
7. **Guest columns.** Pitch Police1 and Corrections1 a monthly custody case law column. Lexipol owns both and accepts outside contributors. Each column links back to the brief.
8. **Podcast guest swaps.** The Briefing Room, LRIS First Thursday, and corrections-focused shows. Offer the custody and juvenile angle they lack.
9. **Case pages for search.** Give each case its own URL and title so "Collazo v. Otsego County training" finds the app. The content already exists in structured form; this is a routing change.
10. **Free chapter.** Offer the first chapter of Force of Law Volume 2 (California) as the email signup gift, replacing the mailto link.

### Months 3 to 6: convert free readers into STC hours

11. **Quarterly STC-certified case law update course.** Compile the quarter's briefs into a 1- or 2-hour STC course with a knowledge check and certificate. Readers already know the material; agencies get credit; the RFC process is documented in Dropbox. Free preview, Solo price, agency bundles.
12. **Agency plan as the ask.** Every roll call brief and every email ends with the same line: preview any course free, agency bundles from $65 per officer.
13. **Conference presence.** CPOC training events, CPPCA, APPA, and PREA audit circles. A 20-minute "this quarter's custody case law" talk is the brief in person, and it books courses.

### What to measure

App installs, email subscribers, episode downloads, roll call brief prints and shares, course previews started, and course seats sold, tracked monthly. The numbers that matter are subscribers and previews; the rest explain them.
