### Short 1: Pitts v. Burke (leaving in place Burke v. Pitts, Tenth Circuit)

- Hook (on screen and spoken): One command followed by repeated Taser cycles without warning, on a person who is not resisting and has not been told he is being detained, was held clearly unlawful.
- Body: Warn when you can, and give people time to comply. Tell the person he is being detained before force is used. Teach officers to articulate the specific hostile motion they saw, not just that the person moved.

### Short 2: Frasure v. City of Wyoming, Ohio (leaving in place the Sixth Circuit's grant of immunity)

- Hook (on screen and spoken): An officer in the path of a moving vehicle is in a very different legal position from one who is not.
- Body: For expert witnesses and reviewers: hindsight is not the standard. Anchor every opinion in the video and in accepted practice, and meet every disclosure deadline to the letter. For officers: position so that you are never the thing a vehicle has to drive through.
- Card: Frasure v. City of Wyoming, Ohio (leaving in place the Sixth Circuit's grant of immunity), Supreme Court of the United States; decision below from the Sixth Circuit, May 2026, Review denied October 13, 2026

### Short 3: Vision Bank v. Avery

- Hook (on screen and spoken): A weapon held down is not a weapon raised.
- Body: Treat civil standbys as potential domestic violence calls: get the history, plan positions, bring a second officer. Against an impact weapon that is not being raised, distance and a barrier are the answer. Train officers to articulate the specific hostile motion they saw, not just that he had a bat.
- Card: Vision Bank v. Avery, Tenth Circuit, October 13, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Vision Bank v. Avery: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 4: Rockett v. Eighmy

- Hook (on screen and spoken): No youth goes into a holding cell or a room without a documented legal basis: a detention order, an arrest on probable cause, or a defined emergency.
- Body: Train staff to stop and call a supervisor when an instruction to confine a young person has no paperwork behind it. Do not assume an instruction from a judge or a supervisor protects the person who turns the key. Build the legal-basis check into the room confinement and court holding procedure.
- Card: Rockett v. Eighmy, Eighth Circuit, October 13, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Rockett v. Eighmy: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 5: Martinez v. Hidalgo County

- Hook (on screen and spoken): The constitutional floor is not the standard.
- Body: Forcing entry to make an arrest can shrink time and distance to nothing. In California, Penal Code section 835a makes those earlier tactical decisions part of the analysis. Train containment and crisis response for a barricaded subject when nobody else is at risk, and train officers to follow the agency's own barricade policy, because a court will notice when they do not.
- Card: Martinez v. Hidalgo County, Fifth Circuit, October 15, 2026

### Short 6: Bailey v. Michigan Department of Corrections

- Hook (on screen and spoken): An environmental grievance is a safety report, and a cover-up is worse than the hazard.
- Body: Route environmental grievances to someone with the authority to fix the problem, document the response, and never paint over a hazard. Supervisors who receive complaints and do nothing are the ones who stay in the case.
- Card: Bailey v. Michigan Department of Corrections, Sixth Circuit, October 15, 2026
