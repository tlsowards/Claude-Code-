#### Short 1.1: King v. Villegas

- Question (on screen, first two seconds): What does King v. Villegas mean on your next shift?
- Hook (on screen and spoken): For custody staff, the headline is simple: in the Ninth Circuit, a prisoner's no contest plea to resisting staff no longer closes the civil case over the same incident.
- Body: Officers and agencies that have treated a criminal disposition as the end of the civil exposure should stop doing so for no contest pleas, and the same reasoning reaches a street arrest that ends in a no contest plea to section 69 or section 148. What follows from that is about reports. King's officers wrote reports that became the factual basis for the plea, and those reports will now be tested against his complaint in front of a factfinder.
- Card: King v. Villegas, Ninth Circuit, en banc, November 24, 2026

#### Short 1.2: King v. Villegas

- Question (on screen, first two seconds): How do you train King v. Villegas?
- Hook (on screen and spoken): Teach the rule in one sentence: a resisting conviction and an excessive force claim can both be true, and a no contest plea admits no facts at all.
- Body: Pair King with Smith v. City of Hemet from the Case library, where the full court said the same thing about a guilty plea under section 148, so staff hear that this is not new; what is new is that a no contest plea no longer carries a factual narrative into the civil case. Then train the report. Walk through the officers' account in King as a sequence and have staff mark every point where force was applied and name the resistance that preceded it; then mark the point where the inmate complied and the force changed to control and handcuffing.

## Community post poll (optional, Saturday)

```
In King v. Villegas, why did the en banc Ninth Circuit hold that Heck v. Humphrey did not bar the prisoner's excessive force suit after his no contest plea to resisting an officer?
A. Because Federal Rule of Evidence 410 made the plea inadmissible in the civil case
B. Because a no contest plea admits no facts, so there was no factual narrative binding him, and his allegations left room for a prima facie basis for the resisting charge at another point in the encounter
C. Because the officers' incident reports were not part of the state court record
D. Because the criminal charges were filed more than a year after the incident

Answer in the brief: https://app.sowardsconsulting.com/briefs/2026-w48
```

Correct answer for the follow-up reply: B. The court did not reach Rule 410. It held that a no contest plea is not an admission of factual guilt, that King was therefore bound to no narrative, and that his allegation of being struck while not resisting did not rule out resistance at another moment. Sanders v. City of Pittsburg was overruled to the extent it held otherwise.
