## Shorts (vertical, under 60 seconds each)

### Short 1: Johnson v. Adgar

- Hook (on screen and spoken): A launcher round that hits the wrong person is not an accident outside the Fourth Amendment.
- Body: Teach the 40mm launcher as a point target tool, never an area tool. The articulation for every round is a named person doing a specific act, the distance, the expected point of impact, and who is behind and beside the target. If the operator cannot identify the target, the operator holds.

## Community post poll (optional, Saturday)

```
In Johnson v. Adgar, why was Johnson seized even though the officer did not aim at him?
A. Because any injury caused by police is a seizure
B. Because the officer intentionally fired into a group Johnson was part of and the round limited his movement
C. Because the officer was ordered to fire
D. Because Johnson was later arrested

Answer in the brief: https://app.sowardsconsulting.com/briefs/2026-w45
```

Correct answer for the follow-up reply: B. Under Nelson v. City of Davis, a person in a group that officers deliberately fire into is an object of that force. The jury found the fire was indiscriminate, and the jury's reasonable reading of the evidence controls after a verdict.
