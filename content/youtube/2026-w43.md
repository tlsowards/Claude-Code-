## Shorts (vertical, under 60 seconds each)

### Short 1: Astorga v. Villanueva

- Hook (on screen and spoken): A sheriff or chief who was not on scene and did not ratify the force is very hard to hold personally liable for crowd control injuries in this circuit.
- Body: For command staff: what happens after a crowd control event is evidence. Review every less-lethal deployment, document the review, and never announce, before or after, that nobody will be disciplined. That is the Larez problem.
- Card: Astorga v. Villanueva, Ninth Circuit, October 21, 2026

### Short 2: Kilnapp v. Gannon (leaving in place Kilnapp v. City of Cleveland, Sixth Circuit)

- Hook (on screen and spoken): In the Sixth Circuit going forward, a bystander, a hostage, or a partner hit by a deliberately fired round has a Fourth Amendment claim judged under ordinary Graham reasonableness, which is far easier for a plaintiff than the shocks-the-conscience test.
- Body: Target identification and backstop are not just range rules. Do not fire at a threat you cannot see. Moving and shooting inside a dark structure with a partner nearby is a crossfire problem.
- Card: Kilnapp v. Gannon (leaving in place Kilnapp v. City of Cleveland, Sixth Circuit), Supreme Court of the United States; decision below from the Sixth Circuit, February 2026, Review denied October 19, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Kilnapp v. Gannon (leaving in place Kilnapp v. City of Cleveland, Sixth Circuit): in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 3: Sheriff of Ottawa County v. Bond (leaving in place Bond v. Sheriff of Ottawa County, Tenth Circuit)

- Hook (on screen and spoken): One in-custody medical death can produce an eight-figure verdict against a county.
- Body: Medical watch has to mean medical care. Moving a sick person to segregation so staff can watch him is not treatment. Document checks with real observations, escalate seizures and breathing problems to a clinician, and train staff that "he's faking it" is not an assessment.
- Card: Sheriff of Ottawa County v. Bond (leaving in place Bond v. Sheriff of Ottawa County, Tenth Circuit), Supreme Court of the United States; decision below from the Tenth Circuit, April 2026, Review denied October 19, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Sheriff of Ottawa County v. Bond (leaving in place Bond v. Sheriff of Ottawa County, Tenth Circuit): in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 4: Garcia v. United States

- Hook (on screen and spoken): Put Garcia next to Kilnapp.
- Body: Train positive target identification before deadly force, especially in hostage and barricade work. A dropped tool or a sudden noise is a reason to reassess, not a reason to shoot.
- Card: Garcia v. United States, Fifth Circuit, October 21, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Garcia v. United States: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.
