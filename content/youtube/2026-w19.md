## Shorts (vertical, under 60 seconds each)

### Short 1: Barnes v. Felix

- Hook (on screen and spoken): Where the entire arc of the encounter supports the officer's perception of imminent threat, the officer wins.
- Body: Use Barnes, Ruffin, and Byers together in one block. Put the three fact patterns side by side and ask trainees what the officer faced at the instant of the shot and what the officer did in the minutes before. Teach tactical patience, distance, cover, communication, and documentation as the levers that make the whole arc favor the officer.
- Card: Barnes v. Felix, Supreme Court, April 27, 2026 (certiorari denied)
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Barnes v. Felix: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 2: Ruffin v. Davis

- Hook (on screen and spoken): The presence of a weapon on a fleeing suspect does not, standing alone, justify deadly force.
- Body: Four points. First, teach deadly force authority on the Garner framework first: armed plus immediate threat is the standard, and foot pursuits should be resolved with distance, communication, containment, and resources, not with deadly force on the theory that armed equals threat. Second, teach that body camera footage is the evidentiary anchor in every deadly force case.
- Card: Ruffin v. Davis, Fourth Circuit, April 29, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Ruffin v. Davis: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 3: Byers v. Painter

- Hook (on screen and spoken): A subject with an edged weapon held at his waist, 25 feet away and walking away from officers, is a subject for positioning, communication, and resource staging, not engagement.
- Body: Three points for the behavioral health crisis posture. First, time, distance, and cover are doctrine, not preference. Ninety seconds is not enough time to manage a behavioral health crisis, and 25 feet is communicate distance, not closing distance.
- Card: Byers v. Painter, Fourth Circuit, Late April 2026 (exact date not stated in the source)

Correct answer for the follow-up reply: B. Under Tennessee v. Garner, armed alone is not enough. The Fourth Amendment requires armed plus an immediate threat tied to what the suspect is doing in real time, and the court reached that conclusion from the body camera footage rather than the officer's later account.
