### Short 1: Manning v. City of Tulsa

- Hook (on screen and spoken): The Garner floor is still the floor.
- Body: Teach deadly force authority on the Garner framework first, then layer on the post-Smith specificity inquiry. Train officers that under Barnes and the totality framework the universe of evidence about an encounter is getting larger, not smaller; body cameras, dash cameras, and helicopter footage are part of the agency's risk posture both for and against the officer. Train officers that whatever they say while a recording device is rolling will be heard by everyone who reviews the case, including what other officers in the encounter say, and train command staff and FTOs to coach officers out…

### Short 2: Boulder body camera fee ruling (Yellow Scene Magazine request)

- Hook (on screen and spoken): Body camera footage in misconduct cases is going to be released, often quickly and at low cost.
- Body: Review retention and access policies so they anticipate fast, low-cost release in misconduct cases. Tighten activation discipline: activate early, leave the camera on through the entire encounter, and narrate decisions in real time. Train officers to use body camera narration to document their threat perception, the alternatives they considered, the warnings they issued, and the medical monitoring they performed.

```
In Manning v. City of Tulsa, why did the Tenth Circuit deny qualified immunity even after the Supreme Court's recent specificity decisions?
A. Because the officer was acquitted of manslaughter, which settled the civil case
B. Because Tennessee v. Garner, a Supreme Court deadly force decision squarely on point, put the unlawfulness of shooting an unarmed, non-dangerous subject beyond debate
C. Because the helicopter officer's narration proved the shooting was unreasonable
D. Because qualified immunity no longer applies to deadly force cases

Answer in the brief: https://app.sowardsconsulting.com/briefs/2026-w20
```

Correct answer for the follow-up reply: B. The court did not need a prior case with identical facts, only one that placed the unlawfulness beyond debate at the level of generality the conduct presented. Garner, a Supreme Court holding on deadly force against an unarmed, non-dangerous subject, needed no further specificity, and the video let a reasonable jury find those facts.
