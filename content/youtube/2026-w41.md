### Short 1: Shelby v. Manning

- Hook (on screen and spoken): The core Graham questions still drive these cases.
- Body: Do not let anyone in a briefing room describe a cert denial as the Supreme Court endorsing or rejecting anything. Use the three Graham questions as the spine of every deadly force debrief.

### Short 2: Navejas v. City of El Paso

- Hook (on screen and spoken): In the Fifth Circuit, a single Taser cycle on an unrestrained person who is throwing things at an officer is very likely protected by qualified immunity, even when that person is elderly and cognitively impaired.
- Body: A man died, and nobody should walk away thinking this went well. With a 70-year-old, 130-pound man with dementia, the fall is the danger. Train that a probe deployment on an elderly person, from behind, while he is moving, carries a real risk of a head strike when he goes down.
- Card: Navejas v. City of El Paso, Fifth Circuit, October 8, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Navejas v. City of El Paso: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 3: Fischer v. Phillips

- Hook (on screen and spoken): The Fifth Circuit gives officers a lot of room when a vehicle is moving toward them at close range, even at walking speed.
- Body: California Penal Code section 835a requires that deadly force be necessary in light of the totality of the circumstances, and most modern policies tell officers to move out of the path of a vehicle rather than shoot at it. Look at the tactics here: the officers boxed the car in and then an officer stepped into the space the car had to move through. Train officers to plan a block before they make it, to stay out of the path of travel, and to recognize that shooting the driver of a moving car rarely stops the car.
- Card: Fischer v. Phillips, Fifth Circuit, October 8, 2026
- Close: Full brief and training points in the Sowards Briefing Room, link in bio.
- Cut from: the "Fischer v. Phillips: in practice" and "train it" segments of the long video, or re-record from this text in the same voice.

### Short 4: Byrum v. Knox County

- Hook (on screen and spoken): In the Sixth Circuit, video showing obvious, prolonged medical distress with no meaningful response can carry a failure-to-train claim past a motion to dismiss, even without details about the training curriculum.
- Body: Intake medical screening happens every time, no exceptions, no matter how busy booking is. Every officer recognizes the signs of overdose and withdrawal. Naloxone is available and staff know how to use it.
- Card: Byrum v. Knox County, Sixth Circuit, October 8, 2026
