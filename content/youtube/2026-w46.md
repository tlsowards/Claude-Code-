#### Short 1.1: Dickinson v. Trump

- Question (on screen, first two seconds): What does Dickinson v. Trump mean on your next shift?
- Hook (on screen and spoken): For agencies in the Ninth Circuit this is the clearest recent statement of when a crowd may be dispersed with less lethal tools: an objectively reasonable conclusion that there is a clear and present danger of riot, disorder, interference with traffic, or another immediate threat to public safety, peace, or order, judged by the demonstration as a whole.
- Body: An imminent threat of injury is not required. That answers the dispersal decision, not the targeting decision. Two weeks ago in Johnson v. Adgar the same court held that a launcher round fired indiscriminately into a crowd violated the Fourth Amendment, and this panel repeated that a claim against an individual officer is a different analysis.
- Card: Dickinson v. Trump, Ninth Circuit, November 10, 2026

#### Short 1.2: Dickinson v. Trump

- Question (on screen, first two seconds): How do you train Dickinson v. Trump?
- Hook (on screen and spoken): Teach the dispersal decision and the targeting decision as two decisions with two articulations.
- Body: The dispersal articulation names the objective conditions: what the crowd as a whole was doing, which laws were being broken, what was blocked, thrown, or damaged, what orders were given and ignored, and why the event had turned from protest to disorder. The targeting articulation for any impact round names the person, the act, the distance, the expected point of impact, and who was beside and behind the target. Build command post logs and force reports that capture both, with times, and keep the warning recordings.
- Card: Dickinson v. Trump, Ninth Circuit, November 10, 2026

#### Short S1.1: Jehangir v. East

- Question (on screen, first two seconds): What does Jehangir v. East mean on your next shift?
- Hook (on screen and spoken): Officers guarding an arrestee at a hospital are doing legitimate custody work, and the court treated a running camera during that work as part of the job.
- Body: The protection here came from what the officers did not do: they did not cause the exposure, did not position themselves to watch, did not comment, and did not share the footage. Every one of those facts is within an officer's control. Policies on when to point the camera away and how exposure footage is flagged, restricted, and retained answer the state law and policy claims this court did not reach.

The decision concerns dispersing the crowd under the First Amendment.
